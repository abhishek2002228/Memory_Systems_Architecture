\section{Overview}
The assignment had two parts: (A) implement SHIP++ as an LLC replacement policy in
ChampSim, and (B) design a policy that beats it. Part A is
\texttt{replacement/ship\_pp.llc\_repl}; Part B, described here, is
\texttt{replacement/custom.llc\_repl}. It keeps SHIP++'s demand-side behaviour and adds
three mechanisms aimed at prefetch pollution and writeback traffic.

\section{Baseline: SHIP++}
SHIP++ combines SRRIP with a Signature History Counter Table (SHCT). Each line carries a
2-bit re-reference prediction value (RRPV, 0--3; higher means reuse is less likely).
SHCT entries are 3-bit saturating counters indexed by a PC-based signature and trained on
a small set of sampled sets. On a demand fill, a signature with SHCT~$=0$ is predicted dead
and inserted at RRPV~3, SHCT~$=7$ is inserted at RRPV~0, and everything else at RRPV~2.

\section{Design}
\subsection{Adaptive Prefetch Utility}
Guiding principle: \emph{prefetches are guilty until proven innocent}. Rather than treat
prefetch fills like demand fills, the policy measures how useful prefetches actually are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a prefetched line is \textbf{useful} if it receives a demand hit before eviction;
  \item global counters track \texttt{apu\_pf\_fills} and \texttt{apu\_pf\_useful};
  \item utility $= \texttt{apu\_pf\_useful} / \texttt{apu\_pf\_fills}$.
\end{itemize}
Prefetch insertion is then set by the observed utility:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
  \toprule
  Prefetch utility & Insertion \\
  \midrule
  $<10\%$     & always RRPV~3 \\
  $10$--$30\%$ & RRPV~2 only if SHCT~$\ge 6$, otherwise RRPV~3 \\
  $\ge 30\%$  & RRPV~2 only if SHCT~$\ge 5$, otherwise RRPV~3 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Demand fills keep the SHIP++ rule unchanged.

\subsection{Region-Augmented Prefetch Signatures}
SHIP++ signatures are PC-based, so unrelated prefetch streams issued from the same PC
alias in the SHCT. For \emph{prefetch fills only}, the signature is hashed with a coarse
physical region tag (4\,KB regions, truncated to 6 bits). This separates streams and
sharpens SHCT predictions without growing the table.

\subsection{Clean-First Victim Selection}
Among the candidates at RRPV~3, clean lines are evicted before dirty ones. This cuts
writeback traffic and frees DRAM bandwidth without changing the prediction logic.

\section{Evaluation}
All runs use 10M warmup and 10M simulated instructions on the CRC2 traces
(bzip2, libquantum, mcf, xalancbmk). Speedups are relative to LRU. Runs are produced by \texttt{scripts/run\_eval.sh} and
summarised by \texttt{scripts/analyze.py}.

APU's gain over SHIP++ comes mainly from prefetch-heavy traces: on bzip2 and libquantum
with prefetching it adds roughly 4 points of speedup, consistent with APU suppressing
low-utility prefetches. Without prefetching the mechanisms are mostly idle and the two
policies are close.

\section{Conclusion}
Treating prefetches as untrusted until they prove useful, giving prefetch streams their
own region-aware signatures, and preferring clean victims improves on SHIP++ in the
single-core configuration with prefetching (1.0715$\times$ over LRU versus 1.0527$\times$
for SHIP++) at a cost of about 26\,KB of state per core. On four cores it also beats both LRU and
SHIP++ in weighted speedup (1.0294$\times$ versus
0.9972$\times$ for SHIP++), with the largest gain when memory-bound mcf shares the LLC with
streaming libquantum.
